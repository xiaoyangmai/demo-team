# Illustrated learning comic

Make the requested principle understandable through what characters do and what changes. Keep the task's topic, audience and output contract; a successful file export is not proof of learning.

1. Plan four beats: an appealing everyday goal, a believable difficulty, a meaningful choice, and its visible consequence. Fix hair, clothing and a few props in one short appearance line and reuse it unchanged. Track where the driving object is, who holds it, and whether it is usable. For scientific comparisons, show the condition that changes and hold other relevant conditions steady.

2. Draft all four prompts before generating. Each panel is one frozen moment that proves its beat: who is present, where, what their hands touch, and the decisive object's count, place and state. Put change between panels, not inside one. Do not narrate a sequence in a panel, and do not leave the same object in both its old place and its new place. A finished change shows the result, not the intention to start. Open with those two moments, then the appearance line and style. State only needed objects, positively and with a count. Use exactly two large panels in one reading order; do not let a four-panel grid override the brief. No words, letters, numbers, balloons or labels, and no names or time words: the model paints them. Dialogue stays in the HTML captions. Never substitute diagrams, SVG, emoji or placeholder art.

3. Generate each new page from text only with `image_generate`, repeating the appearance line. Do not pass `image_url` or `reference_image_urls` for a new page: on this tool that edits the earlier picture, so old poses and props persist. Submit one `image_generate` entry per call. Count every call against the caller's budget. Four pages are the plan; at most one later call may repair a single page, and only if it still fits. Inspect each produced image once with `vision_analyze`: who acted, what the decisive object touches, and whether that panel's consequence is visible. Do not re-query an image for a softer reading, and do not claim a check from the prompt. Spend the repair on the page where the choice or consequence is missing. Pass a source image only when layout and characters are already right, and say what must change and what must stay; if the layout is wrong, repair with a new text-only prompt. If the repair is still wrong, keep the clearer file and record the defect. Copy the bytes and keep the real format extension; do not rename a PNG to `.jpg`.

4. Write captions and alt text only for actions inspection confirmed are visible. Do not narrate a missing beat. Write two application questions with different changed conditions. Each choice is one short action; put the explanation in its feedback. First name the misunderstanding each wrong choice represents. A well-intentioned child could choose it: a useful step at the wrong time, an old rule after the condition changed, or incomplete evidence. Do not use insult, blame or doing-nothing as the foil to one kind answer. Exactly one option is correct; feedback for the others must not endorse them. Compare choices without the answer key: length, politeness and detail should not reveal it. Feedback explains the decisive condition without inventing a fact absent from the page or question.

5. Give one concise takeaway and one safe, concrete action a child can try today. The action should rehearse this lesson's decisive condition or boundary, not a generic first step. Avoid a moral that could fit any story. Use spoken Chinese and the caller's reading limits; do not make the correct choice a paragraph to remove ambiguity.

6. Write `lesson.json` and run the bundled `scripts/render.py <delivery-directory>` from this Skill's directory. It packages the four pictures, captions, quiz, retry/reset and completion state; do not rewrite its UI. Check the generated files and the manifest, then write the caller's result receipt with only checks actually performed, including the real `image_generate` count and image types read from bytes. Do not edit project or profile files or inspect evaluation material.
